# Aim, evidence and interpretation

This report compares **cathedral plan organization**, **church-to-cloister proportions** and **institutional attachments**. It is not a justification for moving or scaling the F5ZP cathedral. We distinguish **project-derived coordinates**, **historically documented examples**, and **inferences**. The user's Salisbury image provides an important plan reference, but we do not treat its pixel dimensions as an architectural survey.

# 1. Physical scale of the existing cathedral project

The current protected F5ZP plan uses **1 project unit = 1 mm** with X running west–east and Z north–south; crossing center X = 0. The western façade is **X = −40.013 m**. The chevet reference center is **X = +20.213 m**. The current experimental Study-D *outer chapel ellipse* extends eastward another **10.433 m**, so the project's provisional west-to-east **envelope is about 70.659 m**. This is an *extent*, not a fully measured finished building length: the chevet outer arrangement is under study.

The selected E5-C3-L1 Monastic House is **30 × 34 m**; its Great Cloister outer covered walk is **20.2 × 22.4 m** with a **13.2 × 15.4 m** garden. The proposed E5-D1/D2 Great Library is **47 × 32 m** (not approved).

## Salisbury as a scale precedent—carefully

A nineteenth-century account, John Britton's *History and Antiquities of the Cathedral Church of Salisbury*, reports a cloister **181 ft 9 in square within its walls** (~**55.40 m**) and an **18 ft (~5.49 m)** walk. This is a historical survey claim, not a measurement drawn from the user's thumbnail. [Historical source](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/89/Getty_Research_Institute_%28IA_historyantiquiti00brit_13%29.pdf). Its plan uses a cathedral much larger than our current provisional 70.7 m extent, so direct copying of the 55 m figure would be misleading. Verify this historic measurement against modern measured drawings before making precise ratios.

**Key lesson:** the cathedral *and* cloister need a common scale reference. Looking at the two as isolated rectangles, especially without roof elevations, is not enough.

# 2. Cathedral plan types and what they teach us

| Historical plan | Verified organizational evidence | Applicability / limitation |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Salisbury** | Elongated cruciform church, major and minor transepts, eastern Lady Chapel, **southern cloister**, and east-side chapter house through an antechamber. Historic Salisbury library above east cloister walk. [Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1023581); [UVA archive](https://salisbury.art.virginia.edu/cathedral.text.uva10298611478251.html). | Useful for **short perpendicular annexes** and plan readability, but our chevet is curved and our lateral aisle system more complex. |
| **Durham** | Cloister south of the nave, attached to service and community ranges; the choir plan and apse organization differ from ambulatory-ended Gothic cathedrals. Roger Stalley reports proportional experiments, including use of the **62.71 m cloister diagonal** in determining the nave length; the external church length is **123.09 m**. [Columbia/Roger Stalley](https://projects.mcah.columbia.edu/medieval-architecture/htm/sr/ma_sr_discuss_dc_design_lay.htm). | Shows the power of **a regulating geometry** and terrain-responsive siting; Stalley's historical interpretation is not a universal formula. |
| **Westminster Abbey** | Nave/choir/cruciform church and **southern claustral buildings**, east-walk chapter house and library, south refectory, later annexes and the Little Cloister. [Westminster Abbey](https://www.westminster-abbey.org/history/explore-our-history/the-cloisters). | Highlights **connected but differentiated** circulation, and controlled thresholds between cloisters and institutional ranges. |
| **Noyon and Laon** | French Gothic church and cloister examples studied by Viollet-le-Duc and Choisy; Noyon's north/western cloister and chapter-house survival demonstrates that an English south-of-nave arrangement is not the only precedent. [Noyon record](https://mcid.mcah.columbia.edu/mapping-gothic/noyon-cathedrale-notre-dame); [Choisy, fig. 16.162](https://archive.org/search?query=Auguste+Choisy+Histoire+de+l%27architecture). | Helps explore **French Gothic support logic** and alternate positions, especially if the project tests the northern grounds. |
| **Gloucester** | Its transept/choir and cloister systems underwent successive remodeling, including important 14th-century vault experiments. [Tracing the Past](https://www.tracingthepast.org.uk/2021/04/07/gloucester_history/). | Warns against copying a historic plan as if built in one phase; helps separate **plan, development sequence, and vault type**. |

# 3. A cathedral-first comparative analysis grid

For each historic plan, record the following **before** copying any dimensions:

1. **Church body:** total length, nave length, nave and aisles widths, transept projection, choir depth, east-end geometry, number of principal bays and source/measurement status.
2. **Support exclusions:** buttress roots, flying-buttress arches, foundations, porch/chapter-house walls and existing entries. A blank area on a general plan may not be free ground.
3. **Cloister:** outer dimensions, garden dimensions, walk clear width, number of arcade bays, position relative to church, and actual connection length.
4. **Attached ranges:** chapter house and vestibule, dormitory/residence, refectory/kitchen, scriptoria/library, infirmary, and visitor functions; indicate whether *the cloister* or *church* forms their north/south wall.
5. **Circulation:** liturgical/private, ordinary community, scholarly public, deliveries, upper-level access, and emergency routes. Label protected versus proposed openings.
6. **Site landscape:** north/south sun exposure, terrain grade, valley or river margins, south-transept court and views of exterior buttresses, western ceremonial approach, and eastern chevet vista.

## Comparative ratios to extract from measured plans

Use a shared unit for every building: (a) cloister exterior width / cathedral nave-and-aisles exterior width; (b) cloister garden dimension / nave bay module; (c) cloister-to-cathedral doorway distance / total cloister width; (d) library footprint / total cathedral footprint; (e) clear walk width after masonry projections / proposed rib-vault bay width. **Do not combine dimensions from unrelated editions or unscaled drawings**; record dimension source, page/plate and whether it is verified, estimated, or inferred.

# 4. The north-side test that our earlier diagrams omitted

A close **north nave** cloister is historically plausible in some settings. Viollet-le-Duc (*Dictionnaire*, vol. III, “Cloître”, PDF p. 419) discusses both north and south attachments. However, placing the principal cloister northward at F5ZP would be an **explicit proposal to revise the E5-C3-L1 site position**, not an automatic improvement.

Compare four candidates in the *same scaled master footprint*: (1) original locked southern detached cloister, (2) **southern attached** cloister with short vestibule, (3) **northern attached** cloister, and (4) northern offset cloister with a short vaulted connector. Hold the main cathedral geometry constant. A first-stage test should show actual outer buttress polygon and 3D flying-buttress roots, façade views, transept court, daylight, and viable chapter-house placement. Actual entry through nave walls cannot be authorized from a diagram.

The Great Library need not be adjacent to the **same** side of the church as the monastery. It can remain a separate but connected scholarly destination, provided the new links protect the spiritual and architectural hierarchy.

# 5. Research findings and priority decisions

**Finding 1.** In current F5ZP geometry, the **47 m Library** is the largest secondary mass relative to the 70.7 m cathedral envelope. It warrants an independent footprint program, not just moving its rectangle.

**Finding 2.** The locked Great Cloister is **20.2 × 22.4 m**, which is not obviously excessive when set against historical cloister precedents. The **30 × 34 m Monastic House rectangle** is an outer program envelope, not an obligatory single masonry block; attachment ranges should be studied separately.

**Finding 3.** Position matters as much as size. Medieval precedents put functional buildings at convenient *cloister walks*, making the church-and-cloister an integrated working structure. Our former 23.8 m detached gap should be treated as a **question**, not as a design virtue.

**Finding 4.** The existing chevet geometry is distinct from Salisbury's east end. A decision to enlarge the cathedral requires a new approved floor-plan pass and full reconciliation of F5ZK-C, F5ZN-A and vertical reference points. Do not change it merely to improve the relative size of the library.

**Recommended research next step (not a construction decision):** establish a matched-scaled north-versus-south cloister placement atlas using **measured church and institutional plans**, then agree on **which parts of the monastery attach to the church** before designing the library's final outer envelope.
