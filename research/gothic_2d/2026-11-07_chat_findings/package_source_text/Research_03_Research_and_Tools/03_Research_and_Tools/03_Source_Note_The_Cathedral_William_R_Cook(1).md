## Findings useful to the current planning problem

1. The schematic Chartres-derived plan is valuable for **terminology and primary church-body organization**, but **contains no attached-cloister geometry and no usable comparative cloister-to-cathedral dimensions**. Do not use it to size the library, monastery, cathedral, or cloister.
2. Cook's account of **Salisbury** distinguishes its English Gothic character, comparatively rapid original campaign, spire, and flat east end. This complements the user's Salisbury plan, but is not a measured attached-building survey.
3. Cook mentions the **early decorative fan vaulting of the Gloucester cloister**. This is useful as a distinct vault-family precedent, but does not make fan vaulting appropriate for the selected F1-H cloister ribs without a separate architectural decision.
4. The course guide is useful for historical **context, terminology and comparanda**. For actual nave-bay, buttress-root and house-to-cloister clearances in F5ZP, continue to rely on the protected project geometry and independently verified measured medieval plans.
