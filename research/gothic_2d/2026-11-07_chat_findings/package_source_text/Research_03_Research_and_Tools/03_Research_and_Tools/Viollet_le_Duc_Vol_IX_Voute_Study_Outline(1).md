## Terminology register — preserve French words

- **voûte** — vault; **voûtain** — a filling vault/small web or curved infill bay; distinguish from decorative panel.
- **arc ogive / ogive** — *in this article*, often a diagonal masonry rib; do not automatically translate as "pointed arch".
- **arc-doubleau** — transverse or separating arch, but inspect whether used radially/concentrically in an ambulatory.
- **formeret** — longitudinal/wall-facing vault arch.
- **archivolte** — opening/bay arch, context-dependent.
- **sommier** — springing block(s) or seat of arches; **tas de charge** — contiguous masonry springing mass.
- **naissance** — springing; **extrados** — outer/upper face of arch; **intrados** — inner/underside of arch.
- **clé / clef / ligne de clefs** — key/keystone, or succession of crowns/keystone-line; inspect whether geometry or stone assembly intended.
- **lierne, tierceron, contre-lierne** — tertiary ribs and linking/stiffening members; retain names.
- **berceau annulaire** — ring/annular barrel vault; **voûte d'arête** — groin vault; **voûte bombée** — domed/bulging vault surface.
- **appareil / appareiller** — layout, dressing, and assembly of cut stone; **claveau** — arch voussoir; **moellon** — masonry stone; **couchis / cintre / cintrage** — sheathing, centering rib/form, and centering.
- **biaise / rampante / gauche** — oblique/skewed, rising/sloping, and warped/nonplanar; these must not be flattened to a generic "irregular".

## Protocol per reading unit

1. Establish exactly which paragraphs and plates fall in the unit (both printed and PDF page), with line-/figure-level locators.
2. Translate critical French passages faithfully. Keep architectural terms in French alongside English glosses and identify uncertainty where archaic terms have multiple senses.
3. Inventory every illustration: number, page, labels, type (plan, section, perspective, épure), depicted building, dimensions/ratios stated, and what is *observed* rather than inferred.
4. Reconstruct the author's geometric operations on paper first: reference planes, construction circles, spring line, offsets, rib center curves, apex and web keystone lines, stone row layout.
5. Separate **primary-source claim**, **drawing observation**, **modern mathematical restatement**, and **engineering hypothesis**. Do not regard a historical author's mechanical interpretation as verified engineering proof.
6. Test mathematical consistency against the diagram and original wording. Maintain a section-specific list of unanswered questions and potential OCR misreadings.
7. Only after the historical section is understood, compare with **existing V13 v0.10** and A4/A5/A6. Record implications or experimental ideas without changing files or approved geometry.
8. Mark the unit as studied only when prose, figures, references, and contradictions have been examined.

## Mathematical reconstruction questions (deferred; not claims attributed to Viollet-le-Duc)

- Convert each source plan into a parameterized boundary graph with pier nodes, wall boundaries, spring heights and construction centers.
- Determine the compatible curved or planar **horizontal projection** of each arch: point-defined circle, compound circles, independent edge, or other.
- Derive pointed arch spring/crown conditions, unequal rise compensation, and continuity of curvature/tangent at architectural junctions.
- Interpret the described **lignes de clefs** and **voûtains** as boundary/construction constraints for patches, while retaining individual stone course direction.
- Distinguish the geometry of a supporting rib from the three-dimensional web resting on its extrados; identify where a conical, spherical, cylindrical, or genuinely warped web appears.
- Check geometric output independently of masonry plausibility: springer seating, courses terminating on appropriate extrados, plausible voussoir orientation, and no forced rib fan.
- Only later investigate a structural model: thrust and load-path assumptions, bending sensitivity, abutment geometry, and tolerances. Historical prose alone is not an FEA validation.
