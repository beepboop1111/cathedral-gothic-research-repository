# Viollet-le-Duc, Tome X — Complete-Volume Analytical Research Report
**Research date:** October 2026  •  **Source:** the user-uploaded 448-page scan of *Dictionnaire raisonné de l’architecture française du XIe au XVIe siècle*, Tome dixième, Paris: A. Morel, 1868.  •  **Geometry:** no modification to F5ZE, V13 v0.10, or A4–A6.

> **Status language.** “Whole-volume coverage” means every printed index leaf has been ingested and filed in scan order. It does **not** mean that the ~500,000-character OCR transcription has been independently proofread character-by-character. Selected key pages and citations have been checked against rendered images. Every target-volume reference is a finding aid until the user supplies the corresponding Volume I–IX.

## Executive conclusions
1. **Tome X is a monument-first analytical index** to the preceding nine volumes, not a tenth volume of treatise articles. Its value is to reunite references to a building that may be dispersed across many architectural articles.
2. **The physical design embodies research annotation.** Printed index leaves on PDF pages 15–441 odd (214 leaves) face intentionally blank *Notes à la main* leaves on PDF pages 14–440 even. The *Avis* (PDF 9–11) explains that users should append commentary and cross-references.
3. **Reference fields are decade/century, tome, printed page, and figure.** The page/figure fields refer to the other volumes, not to Tome X. The index supplies **no substitute for reading those passages and looking at those illustrations**.
4. **The chevet/vault problem is distributed across volumes.** Example index clusters link ambulatory-adjacent subjects such as *chapelles absidales* to Tome II, *piliers* and *construction* to Tome IV, *profils* to Tome VII, *triforium* to Tomes I and IX, and *voûte* to IX (as well as other volumes).
5. **Careful monument identification is essential.** In PDF p. 373, *Senlis* vault → IX p. 515, fig. 29, while on PDF p. 375, *Sens* vault → IX p. 506, figs. 23–25. They are separate buildings; abbreviating both to “Sen…” would corrupt the catalogue.
6. **Comparative geography is broader than France.** It includes sites such as Westminster, Windsor, Worms, Jerusalem, and others. The W index leaf (PDF p. 435) points to Westminster *voûte* IX p. 523, fig. 34 and Windsor *voûte* IX p. 537, fig. 40. Those may be valuable comparisons, but English examples must not silently be treated as French precedents.
7. **The source does not prove a vault-generation algorithm.** There is presently no source basis for changing radial web bulge, spring lines, doubleau paths, or moulded profile sizes in V13.

## Part I — The book, pagination, and how to cite it
The PDF has **448 scanned pages**, title page at PDF 7, the author’s *Avis* at PDF 9–11, analytical-title page at PDF 13, printed index at odd PDF pages 15–441, and facing notes at even PDF pages 14–440. The index closes at **Y** and reads *Fin de la table* at PDF 441; it has **no Z section**. Closing scanned matter follows.
Use references in the form **“Tome X, uploaded PDF p. 373; Senlis — Voûte → Tome IX, printed p. 515, fig. 29.”** Do not mistake printer’s signatures or a scanned-page number for the printed page in the other volume.
The title-page engraving is illustrative. Sampled index leaves are typographic cross-references; the technical architectural figures listed there reside in the indexed Volumes I–IX rather than being reproduced in Volume X.

### The *Avis*, page-by-page interpretive reading
- **PDF 9:** The dictionary format makes entries easy to look up, but one monumental case study can be divided among twenty to thirty separate articles. Our treatment: create a monument dossier in addition to a subject dossier.
- **PDF 10:** The index tracks building components, historical dates, and related figures, but cannot fulfill every research purpose. Facing blank leaves enable custom notes and cross-references. Our treatment: distinguish source data, interpretation, and modern computational hypotheses.
- **PDF 11:** The introduction concludes with a rhetorical comparison between consultable books and absorbing conversation. This is an expression of the author’s hopes for the *Dictionnaire*, not an architectural proposition.
The complete French transcription and English translation are in the already prepared **Session 02: Avis and First Index Leaf** document; this research packet does not replace or revise that transcription.

## Part III — Source-derived thematic architecture map
This is a **map of where evidence may be found**, not a claim that the listed medieval construction principles have already been established. Short French titles remain in the original architectural vocabulary.

### A. Vaults, arch families, and bosses — highest priority
**French index vocabulary:** *voûte; voûtes; arc-doubleau; arc formeret; arc ogive; clef de voûte; travée*.
The most direct vault comparisons indexed in Tome IX include **Saint-Denis IX p. 501 fig. 22, Sens IX p. 506 figs. 23–25, Senlis IX p. 515 fig. 29, Amiens IX p. 517 fig. 30**. Additional leads include **Paris IX p. 512 figs. 26–27**, **Vézelay IX pp. 486, 510** and English comparative examples **Westminster IX p. 523 fig. 34**, **Windsor IX p. 537 fig. 40**. Earlier discussions for the same monuments are indexed in Tomes I, III, IV, and VI.
**Architectural questions to test when source volumes arrive:** Is web curvature described explicitly? Is the profile of a *doubleau* structural, ornamental, or both? How are arches carried to supports? Are irregular/tapering chevet bays actually illustrated? Does a figure show geometry or merely a completed appearance?

### B. Chevets, ambulatories, and radiating chapels
**Vocabulary:** *abside; chapelles absidales; chœur; chevet; sanctuaire*. Volume X frequently uses **chapelles absidales** rather than the exact project term “ambulatory vault.” It identifies many Tome II page leads in the **457–477** region, including Noyon II pp. 457, 461; Saint-Denis II pp. 457, 461; Senlis II pp. 457, 465; Chartres II p. 466; Amiens II p. 474; Beauvais II p. 477. The index does not establish the geometry of the intermediate vaulted passage.
**F5ZE consequence:** these are strong candidates for analyzing plan relationships among radiating chapels, shared piers, aisle bands, and perimeter supports. Wait for original plans and descriptions before converting any proportions to the generator.

### C. Structural reasoning, construction, piers, responds
**Vocabulary:** *construction; pilier; piliers; pile; colonne; chapiteau; chaînage; contre-fort; arc-boutant; clef de voûte*. An index cluster includes Paris **IV p. 158 figs. 91, 91 bis**, Amiens **IV p. 167 figs. 94–98**, Beauvais **IV p. 177 figs. 101/101 bis/101 ter**, and Saint-Urbain (Troyes) **IV pp. 183, 190 figs. 102–106**. These may form a particularly useful figure-comparison run about structural arrangements. The content and argument of each figure are **not yet verified**.
**F5ZE consequence:** potential material for fixing how ribs and transverse arches terminate into responds without unnatural radial flaring, but no source-backed change is claimed.

### D. Flying buttresses, roof support, and outer elevation
**Vocabulary:** *arc-boutant; contre-fort; chaînage; bahut; pinacle; corniche*. Leads include Beauvais I pp. 69, 71, 74 fig. 61; Chartres I p. 65 fig. 54; Paris I pp. 68, 69, 82 figs. 59, 69, 71; Saint-Denis I pp. 59, 66, 74, 82 figs. 55, 73; Saint-Urbain I pp. 76, 80 figs. 66, 70.
**F5ZE consequence:** important for the exterior profile and narrow clerestory/gallery arrangement, but the indexed lines themselves do not state thrust calculations or allowable spans.

### E. Triforia, galleries, circulation and upper levels
**Vocabulary:** *triforium; galerie; tribune; porche; escalier*. Tome IX references include **Noyon p. 280**, **Soissons p. 280**, **Sens p. 286 fig. 9**, **Amiens p. 290 figs. 11–12**, and **Saint-Denis p. 292 figs. 13–14**, plus Tome I examples around pp. 201–205. They form a research trail, not proof of a single historical gallery system.
**F5ZE consequence:** revisit upper-gallery and choir-overlook concepts only after studying elevation diagrams and physical passage conditions.

### F. Moulding profiles and profile provenance
**Vocabulary:** *profil; profils; moulure; boudin; congé; astragale; abaque*. Named monument profile leads include Chartres **VII p. 489 fig. 6 A**, Noyon **VII p. 497 fig. 11**, Vézelay **VII pp. 495, 505 figs. 9 C/D and 17**, Saint-Denis **VII p. 504 figs. 15, 19**, Paris **VII p. 506 fig. 16**, Amiens **VII p. 510 figs. 18, 27**, and Saint-Urbain **VII p. 519 fig. 22**.
**F5ZE consequence:** these pointers may eventually help identify the monuments and functions behind the user’s own hand-traced profiles. Original tracings remain unaltered; no profile is automatically assigned to a doubleau merely because it looks appropriate.

### G. Windows, tracery, openings and ornament
**Vocabulary:** *fenêtre; meneau; archivolte; rose; verrière; vitrail; fleurons; crochets; statuaire*. These appear at many sites and can be organized into opening geometry, wall/arch interfaces, and decorative sculpture. The index links them to a wide range of Tomes; exact architectural conclusions require inspecting target images.

### H. Construction material, masonry and chronology
**Vocabulary:** *appareil; charpente; chaînage; maçonnerie; brique; fondations; dallage; couverture*. The index frequently attributes distinct centuries to particular parts of a building; catalogue dates **per indexed component**, not as a construction date for the entire church.

## Part V — What the complete-document extraction does and does not prove
The reference packet contains **214 separate leaf text files** and one 540 KB combined text-layer extraction. That makes all pages searchable and provides a stable page inventory. The automated classifier also found lines containing relevant architectural terms across many leaves. This is an **OCR keyword index**, not a replacement for photographic collation: column position is crucial, figures often look like letters, and a term may be hyphenated across lines.
The downloadable **Thematic_Hits_Searchable.csv** identifies candidate lines with their X page number so that we can jump directly to the uploaded scan. Counts of keyword matches should not be interpreted as numbers of monuments or numbers of separate architectural facts.
**Examples of witnessed reading hazards:** `Zi57` is likely printed `457`; `9^,95,90,97,98` can represent `94–98`; `VII` may become `VIL` in OCR; **Senlis** and **Sens** are adjacent but distinct. Consequently, sensitive citations are checked against the page image, and uncertain rows are not silently “fixed” by general knowledge.

## Part VI — Questions to carry into the next nine volumes
- **Curved ambulatory web:** What evidence does *Voûte* provide for ribs and webs over nonrectangular or wedge-shaped plans? Are there explicit plan, section, and perspective figures of a single bay?
- **Rib alignment and springing:** Where do *ogives*, *doubleaux*, and *formerets* share a spring, and how do their different curvatures meet at the pier?
- **Upper-gallery support:** What is actually shown for *triforium*, *galerie*, and *tribune* in relation to the chevet’s inner and outer structural lines?
- **Buttress/chapel relation:** Which photographed/indexed monuments show flying buttresses landing at chapels or passing above circulation?
- **Profiles:** Can the user’s existing traced curves be located to an exact *profil* figure and a specifically identified structural member?
- **Construction vs reconstruction:** Which geometric procedures are described by Viollet-le-Duc himself, and which would be new mathematical conjectures in our V13 generator?

## Part VII — Research sequence, preserving source authority
1. **Begin with Volume IX, *Voûte*,** reading the original French and checking figs. 22–30 and relevant later English comparisons against their printed pages. Use the four-monument list as a start, not a substitute for full entry reading.
2. **Study Volume IV construction/support arguments**, including the indexed Paris IV 158, Amiens IV 167, Beauvais IV 177, and Saint-Urbain IV 183/190 group, and every adjoining figure or passage needed for context.
3. **Consult Volume II *chapelles absidales* and related church plans**, using the printed p. 457–477 cluster to reconstruct how actual chapel perimeters and connecting aisle bands were organized.
4. **Investigate Volume VII *profils***, cross-checking actual figure shapes against the user’s hand-traced moulding files; never overwrite, rename, or transform the originals without explicit authorization.
5. **Complete volume-by-volume article coverage** of linked terms in Tomes I, III, V, VI, VIII and the remainder of II/IV/VII/IX as each user-uploaded file arrives.
6. **Synthesize only afterward** into hypothetical algorithms and compare with the unchanged F5ZE/V13 model. Label source-derived geometry, inferred reconstruction, and modern design decisions separately.

## Provenance and limitations
- **Only the user-uploaded Volume X was used.** No other editions were downloaded or consulted.
- **Original French:** *Avis* fully transcribed in Session 02. For the 214 index leaves, a full OCR-layer transcription is retained, with verified representative pages and selected citation rows. It is not claimed to be a diplomatic transcription of every index entry.
- **Illustrations:** The named figures are located in source Volumes I–IX; none has yet been examined. The uploaded Tome X index does not establish construction geometry.
- **Exhaustive reading status:** all 214 leaves are included in an ordered inventory and searchable appendix; selected high-value entries are manually visually checked. Comprehensive line-by-line verification of every reference remains an editorial proofing task, especially for ambiguous digits and names.
- **No design intervention:** geometry, source code, files, scale, F5ZE topology, V13 v0.10, and experimental bays A4–A6 unchanged.
