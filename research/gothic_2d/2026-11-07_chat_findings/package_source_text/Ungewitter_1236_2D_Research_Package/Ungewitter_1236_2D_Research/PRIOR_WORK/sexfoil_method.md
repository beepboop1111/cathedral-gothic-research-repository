# Ungewitter sexfoil — one 30° rotation only

**Source-faithful stage:** six independently fitted lobe centers and radii from the prior six-circle measurement report (unchanged). Connect the measured centers to an approximately regular hexagon and establish their six dividing rays.

**Analytical stage:** construct one, and only one, rotated copy, 30° counterclockwise on-screen (equivalent to -30° when the engraving coordinates increase down the page). Rotate rigidly about the separately fitted regular-hexagon reference center.

**Discovery:** extend all source and rotated circle arcs to complete compass circles and calculate pairwise circle intersections. Of 24 source-vs-rotated crossings, 12 lie near the interior, at a radius around 101–105 enlarged pixels from the center. These make a provisional inner regulating-point ring; this is *not* a historical inner tracery contour. The original visible exterior arc fragments do not, by themselves, establish those inner points.

**Files:** `SEXFOIL_ONE_30_ROTATION_REVIEW.png` (main three-stage visual), `SEXFOIL_30_CONSTRUCTION_POINTS.png` (simplified point network), `SEXFOIL_30_ANALYTICAL_GEOMETRY.svg` (editable analytical construction), `SEXFOIL_30_QA.json` (measurements), `rebuild_sexfoil_30_only.py` (reproducible script). Source crop and original measurements are included in package.

**Paynter context:** The construction-first, dividing-line approach is informed by J. E. Paynter, *Practical Geometry for Builders and Architects* (1921), Chapters II, IV, VI, X, especially the regulating sketches shown near Figure 189 (printed page 155). The particular 30° copied-sexfoil technique and candidate crossing ring are our modern research explorations, not instructions specifically attributed to Paynter or Ungewitter.

No later rotations, no recursive extra layers, no stonework models, no original cathedral geometry changes.
